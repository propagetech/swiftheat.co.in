What changed around you

A short brief on what moved in your market between 2025 and this month. It is not part of the price and it asks nothing of you. We read it while doing the research for the site, and you should have it.

There is no market size in it and no growth rate. We went looking, and what is published for this category does not survive checking: two firms sell the same Indian packaging machinery market at 3.92 and at 11.3 percent annual growth, and three sell the plastics market at figures roughly eighty percent apart. None publish a method. They are listed at the end, with the reason we dropped them, so you recognise them when the next agency quotes them at you. What is here instead is things that happened, on dates, written down somewhere you can check. What we could not verify is in the second table, as a question with the name of whoever can answer it.

Two things you may have been told that are no longer true

Peenya is not a Special Investment Region. It was notified as Karnataka's eighteenth in June 2025, with a development authority and seventy percent of local taxes ring fenced for roads and drainage. In May 2026 the state's Urban Development Department said it cannot be implemented, because Peenya already sits inside municipal limits. There is no authority and no ring fenced money. Anyone advising you off the 2025 announcement is a year out of date.

The machinery compliance order was withdrawn. The Omnibus Technical Regulation Order 2024 would have put BIS Scheme X across industrial machinery from September 2026. It was rescinded by S.O. 239(E) dated 14 January 2026. If a consultant is selling you readiness for that deadline, the deadline does not exist.

Neither of these helps us sell you anything. They are first because they are the two you can check fastest, and everything after them asks you to trust the same method.

The one that moves demand

The duty on imported moulding machines is the only item in that table with a mechanism you can act on. An imported machine of the size your customers buy costs materially more, for five more years, and a listed Indian machine builder confirmed the effect in its own May 2026 filing to the exchanges. Machines assembled in India are specified and sourced in India. That is a finite, nameable list of companies in Rajkot, Ahmedabad, Chennai, Mehsana and Pune, and it is the one finding here that hands you a prospect list rather than a condition.

What kind of business this may be

The best public evidence on Indian heating demand was commissioned by Tempsens of Udaipur for its stock market listing and filed with the regulator in September 2025. It puts India's installed base of heating systems at about Rs 120,000 crore, assumes fifteen to twenty years of equipment life, and takes about five percent of that base as replaced each year. On its numbers replacement is 41.7 percent of the total opportunity to 2031, and heaters are half that total. Its own text on where cartridge and band heaters go names injection moulding, extrusion barrels and die casting.

Read the caveat before using the number. That model was built upward from announced capital spending in refineries, petrochemicals, metals, cement, power and defence. Plastics processing machinery is not one of its demand sectors. Its figures are a floor, not your addressable market, and the consumable market for band, coil and cartridge heaters in plastics is measured by nobody in public. We looked. What exists is heater manufacturers' own blog posts, which we will not cite at you.

The nearest hard number is next door. Contact temperature sensors, which you also make, typically need replacing every twelve to twenty four months. Tempsens reports 32.45 percent of its own orders as replacement, and it sells into refineries where equipment lasts. Yours should be higher, and we are not going to assert that it is. You can settle it in an afternoon, and it is the third item below.

One thing worth naming without a section of its own. Costs are moving against every unit in Peenya at once: that tariff rise, charged retrospectively, on top of a cluster of some thirteen thousand small units whose infrastructure problems are well documented. None of it is news to you and none of it has a remedy we can offer. It matters here only because it removes an option. Price is not the axis you can win on, which is what makes the next two findings the ones that count.

The channel that is currently your homepage

Section two established that your IndiaMART listing, not your website, is what search returns for your own company name, so what happens to that platform matters to you more than to most of its sellers. Its paying supplier count has now fallen for four consecutive quarters, which the company puts down to churn at its lower tier, while revenue per supplier rose nine percent. Its top two tiers are half the customers and over three quarters of the revenue. A platform charging more per enquiry to the sellers who stay, with the smallest paying tier shrinking. That is your tier.

A second mechanism is visible on its own category pages, where band heaters are advertised by the square inch. Those are not market prices and we are not quoting them as such. They matter for what they train a buyer to do, which is to compare heaters on a rate per inch before anyone has asked what the heater is for. This is the one finding here that the site you are approving answers directly: an enquiry arriving through a form that asks for diameter, wattage, sheath and terminal type is not a rate per inch comparison, and it arrives with no broker in the middle of it.

The one local development that helps you

Look at what your own quality page is waiting for: test equipment and criteria for resistance, high voltage, insulation resistance and dimensional inspection; calibration traceability; sheath material and resistance wire records; batch identification. Section two found that not one of seventeen Indian manufacturers publishes test evidence at all. A material testing laboratory at walking distance is the cheapest route from those empty cells to published, third party numbers. No completion date has been announced, so the action is to register interest now and ask what it will cover.

Six things worth doing in the next ninety days

Cheapest first. Not one is a purchase and none of them involves us.

  1. Confirm your Udyam registration and category

    Under the definition that changed on 1 April 2025 you may sit in a lower band than before, which is the higher subsidy tier on quality certification. It matters most for the payment rule: your customers' auditors are checking Section 37(2)(g) before 30 September 2026, and a registered small supplier has leverage on terms while they are. Cost: nothing.

  2. Download your competitor's market report

    Tempsens listed in August 2026, and its filing includes a 103 page industry report on sensors and heating in India. It is free and downloadable from tempsens.com, and we checked the link works. The same data a national competitor paid for. Have one person read it.

  3. Count your own replacement share

    Of your order lines in the last twelve months, how many replaced a heater already running in a customer's plant and how many went onto a new machine. Your own books. It settles whether you are selling to machine builders or to maintenance stores, and it is the only honest way to use the 41.7 and 32.45 percent above, as benchmarks against a number you measured yourself.

  4. Build a named list of Indian machine builders

    The duty on imported machines runs to 2030 and builders assembling here source components here. It is a finite, public list. The reason to do it now rather than later is that those sourcing decisions are being made now.

  5. Register interest with the Peenya testing centre

    Ask what the laboratory will cover, what it will cost a unit your size, and when. Every answer fills a cell on your quality page that currently reads "to confirm".

  6. Get a written read on the appliance order

    Before 1 October 2026. Your products appear to fall outside it, and "appear to" is not good enough to build on. One written opinion settles it either way.

On this brief itself, and its limits. Everything here was read in August 2026 and policy moves, so the dates are the point rather than the freshness. We preferred gazette notifications, government press releases and stock exchange filings over commercial summaries. Gazette numbers were read through legal and regulatory publishers rather than from the gazette itself, so if one is load bearing for a decision, confirm it directly. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is a promise about what the website will earn you. The six questions above are for a tax adviser, a compliance adviser, a customs broker and a government office, which is why each carries a name rather than an answer.
